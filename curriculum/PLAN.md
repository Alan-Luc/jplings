# jplings curriculum plan

Status: checkpoint 1. Plan only — no lesson content exists yet.
Written against `3a35f53` and the schema proposals in `SCHEMA-PROPOSALS.md`.
Learner: one adult, Cantonese L1, heavy prior anime/manga exposure, beginner
production. Trip: mid-March 2027.

## 1. The shape in one page

- **15 units of new content, 4 lessons each, 60 lessons.** Weeks 1–15.
- **4 weeks of consolidation.** Weeks 16–19, no new grammar.
- Each lesson is ~25 minutes. 60 lessons is ~25 hours.
- Available structured time is ~19 weeks × 5–9 h = **~130 hours**, so lessons are
  a fifth of the work. The rest is review, retry, listening volume and roleplay.
  That ratio is the point: 60 lessons is deliberately small, and **nothing here is
  added to increase coverage.**
- One unit = one communicative task, not one grammar point. Grammar is introduced
  because a task needs it, and each unit names the task it serves.
- Five things run as **continuous tracks** rather than occupying units of their own:
  question building, the repair toolkit, listening volume, katakana/kanji recognition,
  and same-session retry. They appear in every unit from week 1.

Pace assumptions: 4 new lessons a week, landing on the longer days (two on a weekend
day, one on a 60-minute weekday). A 30-minute weekday fits review only.

## 2. What is evidence and what is my judgement

Stated explicitly, because the brief asks for it and because the two should not be
weighed the same.

**Established, from instructed-SLA literature:**

- Explicit instruction plus practice beats practice alone for adults, and the effect
  holds for the grammar forms this plan front-loads (Norris & Ortega 2000;
  Spada & Tomita 2010).
- Corrective feedback that shows the correct form outperforms bare "wrong", and
  prompts that make the learner try again outperform immediate correction
  (Li 2010; Lyster & Saito 2010).
- Spaced retrieval produces better retention than massed practice, with intervals
  growing relative to the retention target (Cepeda et al. 2006; Kang 2016).
- Retrieval practice itself is a learning event, not just a test (Roediger &
  Karpicke 2006).
- High-frequency vocabulary and formulaic chunks carry disproportionate communicative
  return early on (Nation's frequency argument).
- Recognising a form is not producing it; production lags recognition consistently,
  which is why the profile separates them.

**My design judgement, not established findings:**

- The exact counts: 60 lessons, 25 minutes, 4 per week, 10–15 words per unit.
- The unit order, and which travel task owns which grammar.
- That the learner should meet conjugation as one system in weeks 3–5 rather than
  spread across the course. It follows from his documented gap, but the week number
  is mine.
- That anime-anchored lessons will click faster for this learner. This is the brief's
  hypothesis and it stays a hypothesis; §9 describes how it gets tested.
- Plain-form-first sequencing (Tae Kim / Cure Dolly framing) over textbook ます-first.
  The rationale is that his input is anime speech and his production target is
  conversation, not composition; it is an inference from his profile, not a finding.
- That 4 weeks of consolidation is the right allocation rather than 3.

## 3. Pace and adaptation

Fixed: 4 new lessons per week.

**Drop to 3 lessons that week** when either holds, checked weekly:

- delayed-check accuracy on the previous week's lessons is below 80%, or
- daily due review takes more than 20 minutes.

**Return to 4** when both recover. **Never drop below 2**, because the trip date does
not move; at 2/week the plan is in trouble and the correct response is to cut from
§5, not to drift.

**Speed up** — 5 or 6 lessons in a week — only when placement or a test-out lesson
shows the learner can already *produce* the material, and only up to the point where
delayed checks stay above 80%. Recognition never justifies speed-up.

Test-out lessons are short: a diagnostic, one or two production items, then straight
to the next lesson. Weeks 1, 2 and part of week 3 will mostly be test-outs.

**Standing weekly shape**, whatever the lesson count:

| Slot | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Daily review | 10–20 min | SM-2 due items, plus the error log grouped by misconception |
| New lessons | 25 min each | As scheduled |
| Weekend roleplay | 20–30 min | Two `conversation` scenarios from the week's units |
| Listening volume | 10–15 min | Unseen clips; the in-app path is blocked (see §10) |

## 4. What gets compressed

Compressed means: present, tested out, and given a short review lesson rather than
taught as if new. Not skipped.

| Existing material | Treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| です / じゃないです noun sentences | 1 test-out lesson (U1 L1) | He already builds these; what he needs is the 私は omission and は/が, not the copula |
| は / を basics | Folded into U1 and U3 | を is only meaningful with a transitive verb, so it belongs with verbs, not before them |
| Numbers, hundreds, thousands, 円, 回 | **One lesson**, not a unit | Audit §3.1: three of unit B's four lessons teach his strongest area. Keep the sound changes (ろっぴゃく, はっせん, さんびゃく, いっかい) because those are real and he may have them wrong; drop the rest to a single listening check |
| Hiragana | Assumed, tested once | Solid per the assessment |
| Katakana | Continuous track, not a unit | Partial per the assessment; needed for signs and menus, cheap to run in parallel |
| Counters beyond 一つ/二つ/〜枚/〜個/〜回 | Deferred to consolidation | Low return per hour for a two-week trip; a misheard counter is recoverable in conversation |

Cut entirely: kanji production, handwriting, typed composition, JLPT ordering,
textbook reading passages.

## 5. The sequence

Columns: week, the task the unit performs, and why it sits there. Prerequisites are
the units that must come first; everything else is a continuous track (§6).

### Phase 1 — Foundation (weeks 1–6)

The gaps the assessment found, front-loaded. Numbers alone do not justify a phase;
these are the things that block every later sentence.

| Wk | Unit | Can-do task | Grammar introduced | Vocabulary | Requires | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | U1 はじめまして | Introduce yourself and ask the same back | です/じゃないです, は vs が (identity), か, も, dropping 私は | 学生, 会社員, 先生, 名前, nationalities | — | Fastest thing to get right and it fixes the two errors the audit found in the existing unit-a; every later unit's roleplay opens with it |
| 2 | U2 何が好きですか | Say preferences and ask for someone's | 好き/きらい/ほしい + が, は vs が with すき, とても/あまり/ぜんぜん, の | 好き, きらい, ほしい, スポーツ, 音楽, 映画, コーヒー | U1 | が in its easiest, most frequent use before it is asked to carry subjects; preferences are the highest-frequency conversation topic he'll meet |
| 3 | U3 毎日すること | Narrate your day and what you did | **Verb groups and the ます stem as one system**, ません/ました/ませんでした, に (time) vs で (place of action), を | 起きる, 寝る, 食べる, 飲む, 行く, 来る, する, 働く, 見る, 〜時, 〜分 | U1, U2 | Conjugation is the gap that blocks production everywhere; it must come before any further grammar, and a daily-routine task is the natural vehicle |
| 4 | U4 〜たい・〜ない | Say what you want and don't want | Stem attachments he already recognises: たい, ない, ましょう, ましょうか; を vs が with たい/ほしい | 飲む, 食べる, 買う, 泳ぐ, 休む, たくない | U3 | Deliberate reversal of the usual order: he knows what たい *means* and cannot *generate* it, so it is built on the stem machine rather than taught as a phrase |
| 5 | U5 今していること | Say what you're doing and where you're going | **て-form as one system** (て/で rules, 行って/来て/して), 〜ている, 〜てください, を vs に (乗る/会う/歩く) | 住む, 待つ, 持つ, 歩く, 乗る, 降りる, 会う, 教える | U3, U4 | て-form is the third stem and completes the system; を vs に needs verbs of movement to be meaningful, which this unit supplies |
| 6 | U6 聞き返す | Ask, clarify, and recover | Question words (なに/だれ/どこ/いつ/どう/どうして/いくつ/いくら), 〜んですか, 〜って何ですか, 〜は日本語で何と言いますか | 何, だれ, どこ, いつ, どう, どうして, ゆっくり, もう一度 | U1–U5 | The repair toolkit is priority 5 and pays out from the moment he lands; it is placed after the stems so he can say "what does 〜 mean" about forms he can already hear |

### Phase 2 — Travel tasks (weeks 7–14)

One task per unit, drawn from Irodori's A1–A2 can-do topics. The grammar is chosen
because the task needs it, not to complete a table.

| Wk | Unit | Can-do task | Grammar introduced | Vocabulary | Requires | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | U7 行き方 | Get from A to B and ask how long it takes | に/で/から/まで in contrast, 乗る/降りる/乗り換える, どのくらい/〜分かかります | 駅, 電車, バス, 切符, 何番線, 乗り換える, かかる | U5 | First travel task, because the airport is day one, and it drills the に vs で contrast that the assessment singled out |
| 8 | U8 買い物 | Buy something and ask the price | これ/それ/あれ/どれ, この/その/あの + noun, いくら, 一つ/二つ/〜枚/〜個, をください, 〜はありますか | これ/それ/あれ, いくら, 円, 高い, 安い, 袋, レシート, ください | U2, U3 | Prices recycle the compressed number lesson in a real task; the counter set is limited to the four that pay for themselves on a trip |
| 9 | U9 レストランで | Order, ask what's in a dish, handle a follow-up | お〜をください, 〜をお願いします, 〜はありますか, service keigo for **recognition only** (いらっしゃいませ, 〜でございます, お待ちください) | 定食, 大盛り, 持ち帰り, おすすめ, アレルギー, お会計, お水 | U6, U8 | Needs the repair toolkit from U6 to survive an unexpected アレルギーはございますか; the keigo is recognition-only because he never produces it |
| 10 | U10 ホテルで | Check in, leave luggage, ask permission | あります/います (existence) with に/が, 〜てもいいですか, 〜ないでください, 〜ています for states | 予約, 荷物, 預ける, チェックイン, 部屋, 空いている, 〜時 | U7, U9 | Existence verbs are a separate system from です and are needed for "there is a convenience store"; permission forms are needed for luggage and late checkout |
| 11 | U11 道を聞く | Follow and give directions | 〜を曲がる, まっすぐ/右/左/角/信号, 〜の先/手前/隣, どのへん | 角, 信号, まっすぐ, 右, 左, 曲がる, 隣, 近く | U10 | The hardest listening task in the set; it needs existence verbs and place に from U10, and its follow-up question (何分かかりますか) reuses U7 |
| 12 | U12 こまったとき | Report a problem and ask for help | 〜なくした, 〜がない, 〜を忘れた, 〜んですが as a softener, 〜てもいいですか review | 落とす, 忘れる, なくす, 壊れる, 届く, 病院, 財布, 携帯 | U6, U10 | Problems are where repair and permission forms collide; placed after both so it can reuse rather than reteach |
| 13 | U13 予定と誘い | Make, accept and decline plans | 〜ませんか/〜ましょう, から (because), つもり/予定, plain form + と思います | 予定, つもり, 暇, 忙しい, 一緒に, ちょっと | U4, U5 | First unit where he produces plain form inside a sentence, which is the bridge to casual speech in U15 |
| 14 | U14 経験と比較 | Talk about what you've done and compare | 〜たことがある, 〜たほうがいい, より/のほうが/一番 | 経験, 一度, 二度, より, 一番, 好き | U13 | Experience talk is the follow-up question he is most likely to be asked as a visitor, and it needs past plain form from U13 |

### Phase 3 — Keeping a conversation alive (week 15)

| Wk | Unit | Can-do task | Grammar introduced | Vocabulary | Requires | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | U15 くだけた話し方 | Understand casual speech and keep a conversation going | Plain form (だ/だった, plain verbs), contractions for recognition (てる, ちゃう, じゃん, とく, なきゃ), かな/でしょ, あいづち, そうなんですか, 〜んですね | あいづち set, そう, たしかに, なるほど, へえ, じゃあ | U13, U14 | Last new-grammar unit, because casual forms only make sense after plain form is producible; it converts a year of anime listening into usable comprehension |

### Per-lesson outline

Four lessons per unit. Every lesson follows the nine-part template from the brief
(anchors → teach → comprehension → guided production → independent production →
personal answers → roleplay → vocabulary → delayed check), with these per-lesson jobs:

| Unit | L1 | L2 | L3 | L4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | です/じゃないです, dropping 私は | は vs が for identity; も | か questions, じゃないですか | ききかえし (すみません, もう一度, ゆっくり, わかりません) |
| U2 | すき/きらい + が | ほしい + 〜んですが | は vs が with すき | とても/あまり/ぜんぜん; の linking |
| U3 | Verb groups + ます stem | ません/ました/ませんでした | に (time) vs で (place) | を + daily routine production |
| U4 | たい | ない (plain) vs ません | を vs が with たい/ほしい | ましょう/ましょうか |
| U5 | て-form rules | 〜ている | 〜てください | を vs に (乗る/会う/歩く) |
| U6 | Question words + か | 〜んですか | 〜って何ですか; 日本語で何と言いますか | Repair roleplay: 3 breakdowns and recoveries |
| U7 | に/で/から/まで | 乗る/降りる/乗り換える | どのくらい/かかります | Transport roleplay: 切符, 何番線 |
| U8 | これ/それ/あれ/どれ; この/その/あの | いくら + numbers recycled | をください; 〜はありますか | Shopping roleplay + counters |
| U9 | お〜をください; 〜はありますか | 注文: 定食, 大盛り, 持ち帰り | Service keigo, listening only | Restaurant roleplay with アレルギー follow-up |
| U10 | あります/います + に/が | 〜てもいいですか; 〜ないでください | 〜ています for states | Hotel roleplay: 予約, 荷物を預ける |
| U11 | 〜があります + place に | まっすぐ/右/左/角/信号 | 〜を曲がる; 〜の先/手前/隣 | Directions roleplay + 何分かかりますか |
| U12 | 〜なくした; 〜がない | 〜んですが as softener | 壊れた/動かない; 〜てもいいですか review | Problem roleplay: 落としました, 届いていますか |
| U13 | 〜ませんか/〜ましょう; から | つもり/予定 | plain + と思います | Invitation roleplay: accept and decline |
| U14 | 〜たことがある | 〜たほうがいい | より/のほうが/一番 | Experience roleplay: 行ったことありますか |
| U15 | plain form; かな/でしょ | Contractions, recognition first | あいづち; そうなんですか; 〜んですね | Long roleplay: 6+ turns, one unexpected follow-up |

## 6. Continuous tracks

Not units, because each is a technique rather than a topic, and each appears in every
unit from the week listed. These are where the 100 hours that aren't lessons go.

| Track | From | What it is | Why not a unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question building | W1, owned by U1/U6/U15 | Every unit contributes at least 2 production steps that build a question he has not seen. U1 owns か, U6 owns the question words, U15 owns follow-ups | The brief asks for it as a track from week one; making it a unit would delay it to week 6 |
| Repair toolkit | W1 (four phrases), W6 (full) | すみません, もう一度, ゆっくり, わかりません in U1 L4; 〜って何ですか and 何と言いますか in U6 | It is used in every roleplay, so it must exist before the roleplays start |
| Listening volume | W1 | Unseen clips at increasing difficulty, answered by meaning | It is time-on-task, not content. **Blocked on schema proposal P1** — see §10 |
| Reading recognition | W1 | Katakana, then the ~150 kanji these units use, recognition only | His trip needs signs and menus; lessons would crowd out speaking. ~10 min/week |
| Same-session retry | W1 | A missed production item re-queues later in the same session for no credit | It is a loop behaviour, not content; it requires the engine work in audit P0-2 |

## 7. Consolidation (weeks 16–19)

No new grammar, no new vocabulary. Volume and repair only.

| Wk | Focus |
|---|---|
| 16 | Weakest-concept circuit: the 12 concepts with the lowest delayed-check accuracy, revisited through new stimuli only. Two long roleplays |
| 17 | One travel task per day as a roleplay circuit: transport, shopping, restaurant, hotel, directions, problems. Each requires a follow-up question he has not seen |
| 18 | Unscripted conversation volume with a human partner where possible; in-app, the longest scenarios in the course. Monthly benchmark 4 |
| 19 | Numbers and prices drill (the sharpest failure mode in noise), repair drill, and one full trip simulation. No new material in the last 5 days |

## 8. Monthly benchmark

15 minutes, taken on the last day of each month (November, December, January,
February; March is the trip). Same procedure each month so the numbers compare.

1. **Listening, 10 clips, answered by meaning.** Natural speed, unseen, difficulty
   tiered 1–10. Score: clips correct out of 10. **Blocked on P1** — until then the
   clips are played from outside the app by a human, and the result is recorded by
   hand in the work log.
2. **Two-minute monologue**, same fixed theme each month (so month-to-month is the
   same task): record, transcribe, then score from the transcript:
   - clauses per 2 minutes,
   - distinct verb forms used (name them: ます, ない, た, て, ている, たい, ことがある),
   - errors per clause, split into particle errors and everything else.
3. **Five-turn unscripted roleplay** with a human partner, five scenario prompts
   rotated across months, all drawn from the course's units. Score: turns survived
   before the conversation breaks down, and whether he repaired it.

Comparison month to month: clause count and turning count should rise; errors per
clause should fall; form count should rise in the order the course introduces them.
A month where errors per clause rise is not automatically bad if clauses doubled —
report both. The benchmark's job is to say whether the pace rules in §3 fire, not to
produce a grade.

**Not measurable in-app:** the unscripted roleplay needs an LLM partner (out of scope
this phase) or a human. This is stated rather than solved.

## 9. Testing the anchor hypothesis

Each lesson is marked `anchored: true` when its anchors are lines the learner has
plausibly heard in anime, and left unmarked otherwise. `SCHEMA-PROPOSALS.md` P5 adds
the field; until it exists the marks live in this document and the lesson files as a
sidecar list.

After about a month (~16 lessons, roughly half anchored), compare delayed-check
accuracy, first-attempt production accuracy and same-session retry success between
anchored and unanchored lessons over the same concepts. If the gap is under roughly
10 percentage points, the hypothesis is not doing any work and anchors stay as a
motivational device with no claim attached. Reported in the work log with the numbers,
not with an impression.

## 10. Blocked and assumed

- **Listening comprehension (P1).** The whole comprehension track in this plan is
  audio-first and answered by meaning, and the schema cannot express it: `listening`
  is dictation-only, and an audio field on an `interpretation` step lints clean and
  plays nothing. Every listening item in §5 assumes P1 lands. If it does not, the
  comprehension track becomes reading, which is the wrong skill, and the benchmark's
  first element stays manual.
- **Teaching text (P3).** `content/teach/<lesson-id>.md` sidecars are safe to write
  today because the loader only reads `.yaml`/`.yml`. Rendering them needs P3.
- **Retry and reveal after a miss (audit P0-2).** The plan's "practice" stage assumes
  a miss shows the answer and re-queues the item. Without it, a lesson is a quiz.
- **Concept sharing across units (P2).** Particles and stems recur in 10 units each.
  Without P2 the coverage report and the review schedule fragment, and this plan's
  claim that items 2–4 of the brief's priority order are "fully covered" cannot be
  demonstrated.
- **Irodori alignment.** Units are mapped to Irodori's A1–A2 can-do topics from
  knowledge of the series, not from the book. Topic-level alignment I am confident in;
  lesson-level alignment I have not verified and do not claim.

## 11. Where the brief and the code disagree

Followed the code, per the brief's own rule:

- The brief expects ~60–65 lessons over ~15–16 weeks "before consolidation". This
  plan has 60 lessons over 15 weeks and 4 consolidation weeks, inside a 19-week
  window. The brief's own pace rule (drop to 3 when accuracy falls) plus ~130 hours
  of available time means the schedule must have slack; holding 65 lessons would
  remove it.
- The brief lists a vocabulary-card kind as an expected proposal. One change covers
  both vocabulary and listening comprehension, so no new kind is proposed. See
  `SCHEMA-PROPOSALS.md` §3.
- The brief's lesson template assumes 6–10 comprehension items per lesson. At 60
  lessons that is 360+ audio items; the coverage rule (3 comprehension + 3 production
  per concept) is the floor I author to, and lessons target 10–15 concepts each, so
  the template's count holds only if P1 lands.
